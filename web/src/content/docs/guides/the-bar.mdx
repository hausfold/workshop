**sill** replaces the macOS menu bar with
[SketchyBar](https://github.com/FelixKratz/SketchyBar) — a fully scriptable
status bar tuned to the [nebelung](/reference/palette/) palette and wired to your
[tiling](/guides/window-management/). When sill is on, the native menu bar is
hidden so the bar is the single source of glanceable state.

## What's on the bar

**Far left — the haus logo.** The pill where macOS puts the Apple menu. It is a
house glyph in your accent colour, it changes colour when something on the
machine wants you, and it opens the system menu on click — see
[The logo pill](#the-logo-pill).

**Left — workspace pills.** One pill per workspace you've
[declared](/guides/window-management/#your-workspaces). A pill shows either the
workspace's own id or its `icon` (e.g. `:slack:`), and the focused workspace is
highlighted. The front app's title sits alongside.

**Right — the status cluster (on by default, each one hideable):**

- **Clock** and **battery**
- **Wi-Fi** state
- **Weather** — a compact pill that opens a full popover on click
- **Media** — what's playing, and the controls for it (see [The media pill](#the-media-pill))

Any of these five can be switched off individually — see
[Toggling bar items](#toggling-bar-items) below.

The pills react live: switch workspaces and the highlight follows; tap the
Caps-Lock leader and the whole bar flips into launcher letter-mode.

## Dropdowns

Several pills open a dropdown on click — the weather popover, the calendar's
timeline of the day, the coffee pill's durations, the agents and AI-usage lists,
and the cpu and memory readouts' breakdown of what's using the machine.
They behave like every other menu on the Mac: **click anywhere else and the
dropdown closes**, clicking the pill again also closes it, and opening one
dropdown closes whichever was already open.

There are no exceptions to the *click* half of that rule any more: **every**
dropdown on the bar, the media pill's included, is on the left click. Each of
these pills then puts its one most-pressed action on the **right** click
instead — play/pause for media, **join the meeting** for the calendar, and
**Activity Monitor** for the cpu and memory pills, on the tab each one is
about.

(SketchyBar itself only ever hears about clicks on its own pills, so the rice
ships a small helper — `sillpop` — to notice the click outside. It runs only
while a dropdown is open, and needs no Accessibility permission.)

## The logo pill

The leftmost pill is the rice's own mark, sitting where macOS puts the Apple
menu. It does three things.

### It opens the system menu

| gesture | what you get |
|---|---|
| **click** | the **haus menu** — System Settings, Activity Monitor, Lock Screen, Nix Config, Haus Settings, Rebuild System, Reload SketchyBar |
| **⌘ click** | `haus rebuild`, straight into a floating terminal |
| **right click** | the full [pounce](/guides/pounce/) palette — the same thing ⌘Space opens |

The menu is drawn by pounce, and each of its rows *is* the palette command of
the same name — so anything you can do from the menu you can also reach by
typing, and there is only one copy of each to go wrong. All three gestures need
`haus.pounce.enable` (on by default); `haus.sill.logo.gestures = false` turns
them off, so the pill is plainly not a button rather than a button that does
nothing.

### It tells you the machine is fine

The colour is a status light — it shows the worst thing currently true:

| colour | meaning |
|---|---|
| your accent | everything the rice runs is up |
| yellow | a newer rice is available — `haus update` |
| red | something the rice runs is enabled but **not running** |

Red is the one worth having. A wedged background agent is otherwise invisible:
AeroSpace stops tiling but leaves every window exactly where it was, and a
stopped bar keeps the last frame it drew on screen — a bar that has died and a
bar with nothing to say look identical. The check is local and free (it is the
same one `haus doctor` opens with), and runs every five minutes.

The **yellow** half is the exception and is **off by default**: it asks GitHub
whether the rice you pinned has moved, which is the only thing this pill does
that leaves your machine. Turn it on with `haus.sill.logo.updateCheck = true`.

<Aside type="tip" title="Hover it">
Resting the pointer on the pill sweeps it through the six hausfold accents —
the same six that turn through the mark on
[hausfold.co](https://hausfold.co). It only runs while the pill is at its
resting colour: a pill that has gone yellow or red has something to say, and
does not also get a rainbow. `haus.sill.logo.sweep = false` if you'd rather it
sat still.
</Aside>

### It changes shape for the leader

Tap ⇪ and the pill fills in — the accent moves from the glyph to the pill
behind it — while the rest of the left side becomes the
[launcher picker](/guides/window-management/). Same glyph, inverted; the status
colours and the hover sweep stand down until you're out of launch mode.

### Changing the glyph

`haus.sill.logo.icon` takes a character, `haus.sill.logo.size` its point size,
and `haus.sill.logo.color` an accent name (left alone it follows
[`haus.theme.accent`](/guides/theming/)). The default is a solid Nerd Font house
at 20pt.

```nix
haus.sill.logo.icon = "⌂";   # the hausfold mark itself
haus.sill.logo.size = 25;    # it needs the extra points — see below
haus.sill.logo.color = "teal";
```

Whatever you pick has to hold up at 28pt with a pill's padding around it, which
rules out more than you'd expect — `⌂` (`U+2302`) is drawn hairline-thin and
does not get heavier at Bold, so beside the workspace pills it reads much
lighter than everything near it. The option's
[reference entry](/reference/options/) lists six that work, with codepoints.

There is no way to point this at an image file, on purpose: SketchyBar draws a
background image left-anchored, at a scale you hand-tune per asset, and applies
no colour to it — so a picture here could follow neither your accent nor any of
the states above, and could not sweep. The rice drew this pill as a PNG for a
while and every one of those was a real limitation of it.

## The media pill

The media pill reads the same system-wide session Control Center does, so it
follows a browser tab as readily as Apple Music or Spotify — and it hides itself
entirely when nothing is playing.

Its glyph and colour say what **kind** of thing is playing: an app it knows gets
that app's mark, and a browser gets video or music depending on whether the page
published an album (every music service does, no video site does). It cannot say
which *site* — macOS's now-playing session carries no URL, and neither window
titles nor artwork nor the session's process id can recover one — so it draws a
neutral video glyph rather than a YouTube logo that would be wrong on a Netflix
tab. If you know that on your machine browser video means YouTube, say so:

```nix
haus.sill.media.icons."browser.video" = "󰗃";
```

Two other habits worth knowing. A long title never scrolls on its own — not on
an interval, not even right after a track changes — it only sweeps when you
hover it, and a sweep is a promise once started: it runs one full pass through
the title and back, no matter how briefly you hovered, and hovering again while
it's still running doesn't restart it from the top. And anything longer than
twenty minutes counts **down** (`-38m`) instead of scrolling a name you already
know, which is the number you actually keep glancing at the bar for.

The gestures:

| | |
|---|---|
| left click | the dropdown |
| right click | play / pause |
| ⌥ click | next track |
| ⇧ click | previous track |
| ⌘ click | jump to whatever is making the noise — the tab, not just the app |
| scroll | seek ±10s |
| hover | sweep a long title once, start to finish |

That ⌘ click is the one to remember: it's the answer to "something is making
noise and I can't find the tab", and it means that literally — it puts the
**tab** in front, not just the browser. There's no URL in what macOS hands the
bar (see above), so it matches on the track's title instead, and how it gets
there depends on the browser: Safari and the Chromium family hand out their tab
list to AppleScript, so it's a real lookup. Firefox and its forks — Zen
included — hand out nothing at all, not to AppleScript and not through
accessibility either, so the route there is the one piece of switch-to-tab
machinery Firefox itself ships: the address bar's `%` open-tab search, driven
for you. Each route asks macOS for something the first time you use it —
**Automation** for the scriptable browsers, **Accessibility** for the Firefox
forks — and if you say no, the gesture quietly does what it always did and just
brings the browser forward.

If you're on Zen, there's a better answer than driving its address bar:

```nix
haus.zen.tabBridge.enable = true;
```

That deploys a small extension the rice builds itself, which publishes Zen's
open tabs to the bar and switches to one on request. ⌘ click then gets an exact
tab — across windows, Zen workspaces and Spaces — with no synthetic typing and
no Accessibility permission. It's off by default because force-installing an
add-on into your browser isn't a thing a rice should do unasked, and it's Zen
only for a reason that isn't a preference: release Firefox refuses any extension
Mozilla hasn't signed, and Zen ships `xpinstall.signatures.required = false` as
a built-in default, so the rice can build the `.xpi` and install it straight
from the nix store. The extension asks for one permission, `tabs`, and talks to
exactly one program — a native-messaging host that lives and dies with the
browser, opens no port, and is named in the manifest as the only thing it will
speak to.

<Aside type="note">
Policy files are read once, at startup — so the first rebuild that turns this
on doesn't do anything visible until you **quit and reopen Zen**.
</Aside>

The dropdown carries the cover art when the source published one, the title,
artist and album, and a position slider you can **click to scrub**, plus
transport rows. A title or album long enough to overflow the dropdown sweeps
once when it opens, the same marquee the pill itself does on hover. Sources
that publish no cover — most browser tabs among them — get a small app-icon
badge floating in the dropdown's bottom-right corner: enough to say where the
sound is coming from, without a cover-sized stand-in at the top or a row of its
own in the middle of the ones you actually click.

Two options tune it, both off by default:
`haus.sill.media.collapse` draws the pill as its glyph alone until you hover it
(worth having on a MacBook, where the bar's centre span is under the notch), and
`haus.sill.media.artworkTint` colours the glyph from the current cover — sampled
and then snapped to the nearest colour in your palette, so an album cover can
never put a colour on the bar that isn't part of your theme.

A third sets how much room the title gets before it starts sweeping:

```nix
haus.sill.media.width = 16;   # characters, not pixels. 25 by default.
```

It's a **maximum**, not a fixed size — a short title still draws a short pill,
so widening it costs nothing until something long plays. `collapse` is the
blunter version of the same trade.

## The weather popover

Click the weather pill and a popover blooms open with the full picture:
location, condition, current and feels-like temperature, sunrise/sunset, wind,
humidity, UV, an hourly strip, and a three-day forecast. It's one of the bar's
nicer flourishes and a good demo of what SketchyBar can do.

## Toggling bar items

Every right-side pill is a single boolean in `haus.sill.items` (or in
`haus.sill.bottom.items`, if you've given it [a second bar](#a-second-bar-along-the-bottom)).
The five core pills (`clock`, `weather`, `media`, `battery`, `wifi`) default
**on**; the extras below default **off**. Set only the ones you want to change:

```nix
haus.sill.items = {
  weather = false;      # drop a default-on core pill
  cpu = true;           # add an off-by-default readout
  caffeinate = true;    # add the keep-awake controller
};
```

A pill set `false` is never created — its update script doesn't even run.

**Extra readouts** — harmless anywhere, just off so the default bar stays quiet:

- **`cpu`** — total CPU load, drawn as a **graph pill**: the last two minutes of
  it behind the number. A percentage on its own can't tell you whether 60% is a
  spike settling or a climb that started five minutes ago, which is the actual
  question you glance up to ask.

  **Left-click** opens a dropdown: the user/system split with the load average
  beside it — user-heavy is your build running, system-heavy is the machine
  thrashing, and the load says whether anything is queued behind either — then
  what's responsible, biggest first and aggregated per app, so a browser's
  twenty helper processes are one row rather than twenty; clicking a row focuses
  that app's window. **Right-click** opens Activity Monitor on its CPU tab.

  Nothing happens on **hover**, deliberately. The breakdown used to swap into
  the pill's label while the pointer sat on it, and a longer label is a wider
  pill: every pill to its left jumped sideways on the way in and back on the way
  out. The dropdown opens *below* the bar and moves nothing.

  The rows can only cover processes you own — macOS doesn't hand out another
  user's CPU times — so anything root runs (`kernel_task`, `WindowServer`) lands
  in `everything else` rather than going quietly missing from the sum. A large
  `everything else` is the dropdown telling you, correctly, that the answer is
  in Activity Monitor and not in this list.
- **`memory`** — memory in use, drawn as a **graph pill**. It counts what
  Activity Monitor counts — app memory, wired and compressed — and deliberately
  *not* the file cache. macOS fills otherwise-idle RAM with cache on purpose and
  hands it straight back under pressure, so counting it gives a readout that
  sits near 90% on a machine doing nothing and has no range left to show
  anything with.

  Its **colour is the kernel's own pressure level** (green normal, amber
  warning, red critical), not the percentage: 60% of RAM in use is a Mac working
  correctly, and a pill that goes amber for that is a pill you learn to ignore.
  **Left-click** opens a dropdown with used/total, the
  cache, compressed and swap figures, then the biggest footprints per app —
  the same `phys_footprint` Activity Monitor's Memory column shows, so the two
  agree — each row clicking through to that app's window. **Right-click** opens
  Activity Monitor on its Memory tab. Hover does nothing here either, for the
  reason the CPU pill gives above.
- **`volume`** — output volume / mute state.
- **`calendar`** — the one meeting you have to be at next, and one gesture to
  join it. It reads `in 12m · Design review`: countdown first, because a label
  is clipped from the *end* and the number is the part you can never afford to
  lose. Under `haus.sill.calendar.preciseUnder` hours (12 by default) it carries
  minutes; above that it just says `in 14h` or `in 2d`, because a number you're
  reading as "not yet" doesn't need them. While an event is running it says
  `now · …` rather than going blank.

  For `haus.sill.calendar.imminent` minutes either side of the start — five
  before and five after, by default — the **whole pill fills** with the accent
  and the type goes dark. That is a shape change rather than a colour change, so
  it catches the eye you aren't pointing at it, and it is tied to the *start*
  rather than to the whole meeting: five minutes before is "go now", five after
  is "you're late", and they are the same fact.

  **Right-click joins.** It opens the event's conferencing link, found in the
  invite's url, location or notes — Meet, Zoom, Teams, Webex, Jitsi, Whereby,
  Chime, BlueJeans, GoTo, Around and Discord out of the box, with
  `haus.sill.calendar.joinHosts` for your own. Matching is on the *host*, and a
  bare name covers its subdomains (`zoom.us` catches `us02web.zoom.us`): a
  substring search for "meet" would open the `tel.meet` dial-in page that every
  Google invite also carries.

  **Left-click opens the day as a timeline** — `Done` (the last
  `haus.sill.calendar.past` hours), `Now`, and `Next` (up to
  `haus.sill.calendar.upcoming` events). Each event gets its day and clock time
  in one column, its title in the next, and a dim line underneath with how long
  it runs and who it's with; the event the pill is about is drawn in a box, and
  any event with a conferencing link carries a `Join` you can click. Your own
  address is left out of the "with" line without you configuring anything — a
  CalDAV calendar is named for the account it syncs, so the pill works out which
  attendee is you from the calendar list (`haus.sill.calendar.me` covers the
  cases where it can't, like a local calendar or an alias you're invited under).

  A name too long for the pill sweeps past rather than being cut off with an
  ellipsis, but **only while you hover it** — nothing in this pill starts moving
  on its own. `haus.sill.calendar.width` sets how much room it gets before that
  applies, in characters (32 by default), and `haus.sill.calendar.refresh` how
  often it re-reads the calendar (15s; hovering forces a read regardless, which
  is the case that matters). Pulls in `ical-buddy` automatically and reads
  Calendar, so macOS prompts for Calendar access on first run.
- **`caffeinate`** — a coffee pill for preventing idle system sleep. Click it
  to choose 1, 2, 4, or 8 hours, enter a custom number of hours, keep the Mac
  awake until stopped, or allow sleep again; right-click stops immediately.
  While active, the pill shows the time remaining. The assertion is owned by
  launchd, not SketchyBar, so it survives a bar reload and resumes with the
  correct remaining time after a rebuild or login. It uses macOS's built-in
  `caffeinate`—no package or permission prompt. The display can still turn off,
  and closing a MacBook lid still sleeps it.

The same keep-awake engine is available without the pill:

```sh
awake 3h
awake 90m
awake indefinitely
awake status
awake off
```

**Personal extras** — each targets one person's hardware, service, or workflow:

- **`agents`** — a paw pill tracking your agent-worktree panes: amber when one is
  waiting on you, click for the per-agent list, click a row to jump to its pane.
  Each row is marked with the client sitting in it (Claude Code, Codex,
  Opencode), painted in that pane's state colour, and a row clears itself once
  its pane is gone — however the agent ended. Dormant until a client's hooks
  fire, so it's harmless if you don't use agents. The same state also shows up
  **inside** the terminal: a count badge beside the zellij tab name of whichever
  tab holds agent panes — see
  [Coding agents](/guides/claude-agents/#which-tab-wants-you).
- **`aiUsage`** (was `claudeUsage`, still accepted) — a gauge pill showing how
  much of your AI usage you've spent: a percentage for subscription limits
  (Claude Code, Codex), or the day's dollars for token-billed Opencode. It
  follows whichever provider reported most recently, showing the higher of the
  5-hour or weekly window and colouring to match (green under 50%, yellow past
  50%, peach past 75%, red at 90%). A dollar figure is drawn blue instead: a
  spend has no ceiling to be 90% of, so it can't honestly be "green as in
  plenty left".

  Click it for every provider's windows, each with a ten-cell meter, its reset
  time, and the same colour ladder on the number — so the row you opened the
  pill to read looks the way the pill did. The dropdown holds one rule worth
  knowing, because everything else follows from it: **a colour on a mark means
  identity, a colour on a number means state.** The glyph beside each provider
  is its brand accent (Claude, Codex, whatever Opencode is pointed at), drawn
  from the half of the palette the ladder never uses; nothing but a value is
  ever green or red. A provider whose feed has gone quiet greys out as a whole
  block — mark, name and figures — rather than leaving numbers behind that
  nothing has checked in hours, with an `as of 4d 20h ago` footnote saying how
  long.

  Where each row comes from matters, because one of them leaves the machine:

  - **Claude Code** rides its own statusline feed. Nothing is polled and no
    token is read, so the row appears once a Claude session first reports and
    greys out if none has in five minutes.
  - **Opencode** is read straight out of its local sqlite db. Also no network.
  - **Codex** is the exception. It keeps no usage data on disk at all — the
    desktop app holds threads server-side, and only the CLI and the VS Code
    extension ever wrote rate limits locally — so a disk-fed row silently
    freezes the day you switch to the GUI app. That row is polled from your
    ChatGPT account instead (limits are per account, so it counts GUI usage
    too), authenticated with the OAuth token already in `~/.codex/auth.json`.
    The refresher renews that token when it nears expiry and rewrites the file
    in place, keeping a `.bak`. **If you've never logged into Codex on this
    machine, no call is ever made** — the presence of `auth.json` is the entire
    opt-in.

  Claude's row is *pushed* by its statusline; the Codex and Opencode rows are
  *pulled*, and the pill pulls them itself every few minutes. That matters if
  you don't use Claude: those two rows used to move only while a Claude
  statusline was rendering somewhere, so on a Codex or Opencode machine the pill
  would grey out and stay grey.

  Claude and Opencode each get a **`tokens`** block in that dropdown — raw
  tokens moved today, this week, this month and all time, two periods to a line
  so a full set reads as a 2×2 — and the dropdown closes with an `∑ Everything`
  block adding up every provider:

  ```
  ✳ Claude
    session   48%  █████▁▁▁▁▁  ·  resets 11:50
    weekly    13%  █▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁  ·  resets Sat 03:00
    tokens   220M d  ·  2.02B w
             590M m  ·  7.65B total
  …
  ∑ Everything
    tokens   220M d  ·  2.09B w
             597M m  ·  7.89B total
  ```

  It exists purely for the fun of watching the number climb. Every other figure
  in the pill is a fraction of something you're allowed; this is the count of
  what you've actually moved, which no client shows you anywhere. It's a score,
  not a limit — nothing warns on it, nothing throttles on it, it never reaches
  the pill's own label, which keeps showing the number you can act on, and it's
  drawn a shade quieter than the meters above it, uncoloured and unbolded,
  because a count with no ceiling can't be urgent and colouring it would make
  the ladder mean less.
  Expect it to be enormous: it counts cache reads alongside real input and
  output, and cache reads are most of what an agent session does.

  A period with nothing in it is dropped rather than printed as a zero, so the
  block just gets smaller — first thing on a Tuesday morning you'll see
  `78.8M w · 7.34M m` over `238M total`, and a provider you haven't used at all
  gets no block. The periods are counted independently, so `w` being *larger*
  than `m` is normal and correct: a week that started in the previous month has
  days in it that this month doesn't. `∑ Everything` appears only when more than
  one provider is reporting — with a single feed it would just restate the block
  above it.

  Claude's is summed from your own transcripts in `~/.claude/projects` — the
  only place the raw counters exist. Reading all of them takes seconds, so an
  index remembers each transcript's size and only re-reads the sessions that
  grew; the score updates roughly every quarter of an hour. Codex has no row
  here for the same reason its limits are polled: no local history to count.

  It is not a bill. Nothing de-duplicates, so a resumed or forked session counts
  the messages it inherited more than once.
- **`elgato`** — a pill that toggles an Elgato Key Light on your local network.
  The light is found for you over mDNS and remembered, so one that takes a new
  address just comes back; pin it with `haus.sill.elgato.host` if you have
  more than one light or mDNS is unreliable on your network. A light it cannot
  reach at all — asleep, off the wifi, flat battery — draws **dim**, which is
  deliberately not the same as the pill's "switched off" look.
- **`harvest`** — a [Harvest](https://www.getharvest.com/) time-tracking pill.
  It reads credentials from a file **you** provide at
  `~/.config/sketchybar/harvest_secrets.sh`.

The **hush** (Do-Not-Disturb) pill is separate: it rides
[`haus.hush.enable`](/guides/hush/), not `sill.items`. It can still move to the
second bar with `haus.sill.bottom.items.hush = true`.

<Aside type="note" title="Secrets stay out of Nix">
sill never bakes tokens into the store. The harvest plugin sources your
`harvest_secrets.sh` at runtime — keep that file out of git. `aiUsage` reads
your Codex login from `~/.codex/auth.json` the same way: at runtime, from the
file OpenAI's own tooling wrote, never through Nix.
</Aside>

## A second bar along the bottom

One bar is the default. If you want more room — or you'd rather keep the
readouts off the menu bar entirely — sill can draw a **second bar along the
bottom of the screen**, running at the same time as the top one:

```nix
haus.sill.bottom = {
  enable = true;
  items = { weather = "left"; media = "center"; hush = "right"; };
};
```

A pill named in `bottom.items` **moves**: it's drawn on the bottom bar and not
on the menu bar, whatever `haus.sill.items` says about it. One switch per pill
per bar, and never two copies of the same readout racing each other.

The set includes the five core pills — `clock`, `weather`, `media`, `battery`,
`wifi` — and the extras: `cpu`, `memory`, `volume`, `calendar`, `caffeinate`,
`agents`, `aiUsage`, `elgato`, `harvest`. Hush can move too when
`haus.hush.enable` is on. The whole left side (workspace pills, front app, the
leader picker) and the tour stay on the menu bar.

### Left, center, right

The value is where the pill goes: `"left"`, `"center"` or `"right"`. `true` is
shorthand for `"right"`, so a bar written before the groups existed keeps
drawing exactly what it did.

```nix
haus.sill.bottom.items = {
  agents = "left";
  aiUsage = "left";
  media = "center";
  clock = "right";
  battery = "right";
  cpu = true;        # same as "right"
};
```

The menu bar has only one group to offer — its left is the workspace pills and
the front app, and its middle is under the notch on a MacBook. The bottom strip
has neither, so all three of SketchyBar's regions are yours.

Within a group the order is fixed (the same order the menu bar uses), and each
group packs outward from its own edge: on the right the first pill sits furthest
right, exactly like the clock up top, while the left group fills rightward from
the left edge and the center group grows around the middle of the screen.

Three things worth knowing before you turn it on:

- **macOS reserves the top of your screen for the menu bar and reserves nothing
  at the bottom.** prowl carves the room out instead, so tiled windows stop
  above the new bar — but with `haus.prowl.enable = false` nothing does, and your
  windows will run underneath it.
- **The Dock shares that edge.** Move it to a side, or leave it hidden.
- **Don't put both bars on the same edge.** `haus.sill.position = "bottom"` with
  a second bar means two bars stacked in one place; the rice warns you on
  rebuild rather than quietly drawing one over the other.

Under the hood it really is a second program: SketchyBar draws exactly one bar
per process and names each instance after the binary it was launched as, so the
rice launches the same binary a second time under the name `sill-bottom`. That
name is also how you poke it by hand — `sill-bottom --set cpu label=hello`,
where the top bar takes `sketchybar --set`.

## Turning it off

```nix
haus.sill.enable = false;
```

With sill disabled, the native macOS menu bar is shown again (the rice only
hides it when sill is on) — and the bottom bar goes with it, whatever
`haus.sill.bottom.enable` says. Tiling and the rest of the rice are unaffected.

## Under the hood

sill is generated entirely from your config. On rebuild it writes
`~/.config/sketchybar/` — the main `sketchybarrc`, a `colors.sh` exporting the
nebelung palette as `0xAARRGGBB` values, a `workspaces.sh` built from your app
roster, and a `plugins/` directory of small shell scripts (one per bar item).
The palette and your roster are the single sources of truth, so the bar always
matches the rest of the system.

With a second bar there's a `sill-bottomrc` beside the first rc, and a `bar.sh`
that tells each shared plugin which of the two bars it's talking to — the pills
themselves are the same scripts and each is drawn only on the bar that owns it.
