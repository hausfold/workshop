**sill** replaces the macOS menu bar with
[SketchyBar](https://github.com/FelixKratz/SketchyBar) — a fully scriptable
status bar tuned to the [nebelung](/reference/palette/) palette and wired to your
[tiling](/guides/window-management/). When sill is on, the native menu bar is
hidden so the bar is the single source of glanceable state.

## What's on the bar

**Left — workspace pills.** One pill per workspace you've
[declared](/guides/window-management/#your-workspaces). A pill shows either the
workspace's own id or its `icon` (e.g. `:slack:`), and the focused workspace is
highlighted. The front app's title sits alongside.

**Right — the status cluster (on by default, each one hideable):**

- **Clock** and **battery**
- **Wi-Fi** state
- **Weather** — a compact pill that opens a full popover on click
- **Media** — what's playing, and the controls for it (see [The media pill](#the-media-pill))

Any of these five can be switched off individually — see
[Toggling bar items](#toggling-bar-items) below.

The pills react live: switch workspaces and the highlight follows; tap the
Caps-Lock leader and the whole bar flips into launcher letter-mode.

## Dropdowns

Several pills open a dropdown on click — the weather popover, the calendar's next
five events, the coffee pill's durations, the agents and AI-usage lists. They
behave like every other menu on the Mac: **click anywhere else and the dropdown
closes**, clicking the pill again also closes it, and opening one dropdown closes
whichever was already open.

The media pill is the one exception to the *click* half of that rule: its left
click is play/pause, so its dropdown is on **right**-click. Everything about
dismissing it is the same.

(SketchyBar itself only ever hears about clicks on its own pills, so the rice
ships a small helper — `sillpop` — to notice the click outside. It runs only
while a dropdown is open, and needs no Accessibility permission.)

## The media pill

The media pill reads the same system-wide session Control Center does, so it
follows a browser tab as readily as Apple Music or Spotify — and it hides itself
entirely when nothing is playing.

Its glyph and colour say what **kind** of thing is playing: an app it knows gets
that app's mark, and a browser gets video or music depending on whether the page
published an album (every music service does, no video site does). It cannot say
which *site* — macOS's now-playing session carries no URL, and neither window
titles nor artwork nor the session's process id can recover one — so it draws a
neutral video glyph rather than a YouTube logo that would be wrong on a Netflix
tab. If you know that on your machine browser video means YouTube, say so:

```nix
haus.sill.media.icons."browser.video" = "󰗃";
```

Two other habits worth knowing. A title scrolls for a few seconds after a track
changes and then settles, so nothing moves in the corner of your eye forever —
hover to read the whole thing again. And anything longer than twenty minutes
counts **down** (`-38m`) instead of scrolling a name you already know, which is
the number you actually keep glancing at the bar for.

The gestures:

| | |
|---|---|
| left click | play / pause |
| right click | the dropdown |
| ⌥ click | next track |
| ⇧ click | previous track |
| ⌘ click | bring the app the sound is coming from forward |
| scroll | seek ±10s |

That ⌘ click is the one to remember: it's the answer to "something is making
noise and I can't find the tab".

The dropdown carries the cover art (or, for the many sources that publish none,
the source app's own icon), the title, artist and album, a position slider you
can **click to scrub**, transport rows, and a short **recently played** list.
That last one exists because macOS keeps no now-playing history at all — nothing
on the machine can tell you what the track before this one was, so the bar
writes each change down as it happens.

Two options tune it, both off by default:
`haus.sill.media.collapse` draws the pill as its glyph alone until you hover it
(worth having on a MacBook, where the bar's centre span is under the notch), and
`haus.sill.media.artworkTint` colours the glyph from the current cover — sampled
and then snapped to the nearest colour in your palette, so an album cover can
never put a colour on the bar that isn't part of your theme.

## The weather popover

Click the weather pill and a popover blooms open with the full picture:
location, condition, current and feels-like temperature, sunrise/sunset, wind,
humidity, UV, an hourly strip, and a three-day forecast. It's one of the bar's
nicer flourishes and a good demo of what SketchyBar can do.

## Toggling bar items

Every right-side pill is a single boolean in `haus.sill.items` (or in
`haus.sill.bottom.items`, if you've given it [a second bar](#a-second-bar-along-the-bottom)).
The five core pills (`clock`, `weather`, `media`, `battery`, `wifi`) default
**on**; the extras below default **off**. Set only the ones you want to change:

```nix
haus.sill.items = {
  weather = false;      # drop a default-on core pill
  cpu = true;           # add an off-by-default readout
  caffeinate = true;    # add the keep-awake controller
};
```

A pill set `false` is never created — its update script doesn't even run.

**Extra readouts** — harmless anywhere, just off so the default bar stays quiet:

- **`cpu`** — total CPU load, as a percentage pill.
- **`memory`** — memory-pressure percentage pill.
- **`volume`** — output volume / mute state.
- **`calendar`** — your next timed event, with a click-popup of the next five.
  Pulls in `ical-buddy` automatically and reads Calendar, so macOS prompts for
  Calendar access on first run.
- **`caffeinate`** — a coffee pill for preventing idle system sleep. Click it
  to choose 1, 2, 4, or 8 hours, enter a custom number of hours, keep the Mac
  awake until stopped, or allow sleep again; right-click stops immediately.
  While active, the pill shows the time remaining. The assertion is owned by
  launchd, not SketchyBar, so it survives a bar reload and resumes with the
  correct remaining time after a rebuild or login. It uses macOS's built-in
  `caffeinate`—no package or permission prompt. The display can still turn off,
  and closing a MacBook lid still sleeps it.

The same keep-awake engine is available without the pill:

```sh
awake 3h
awake 90m
awake indefinitely
awake status
awake off
```

**Personal extras** — each targets one person's hardware, service, or workflow:

- **`agents`** — a paw pill tracking your agent-worktree panes: amber when one is
  waiting on you, click for the per-agent list, click a row to jump to its pane.
  Each row is marked with the client sitting in it (Claude Code, Codex,
  Opencode), painted in that pane's state colour, and a row clears itself once
  its pane is gone — however the agent ended. Dormant until a client's hooks
  fire, so it's harmless if you don't use agents. The same state also shows up
  **inside** the terminal: a count badge beside the zellij tab name of whichever
  tab holds agent panes — see
  [Coding agents](/guides/claude-agents/#which-tab-wants-you).
- **`aiUsage`** (was `claudeUsage`, still accepted) — a gauge pill showing how
  much of your AI usage you've spent: a percentage for subscription limits
  (Claude Code, Codex), or the day's dollars for token-billed Opencode. It
  follows whichever provider reported most recently, showing the higher of the
  5-hour or weekly window and colouring to match (green under 50%, yellow past
  50%, peach past 75%, red at 90%). Click it for every provider's windows with
  their reset times.

  Where each row comes from matters, because one of them leaves the machine:

  - **Claude Code** rides its own statusline feed. Nothing is polled and no
    token is read, so the row appears once a Claude session first reports and
    greys out if none has in half an hour.
  - **Opencode** is read straight out of its local sqlite db. Also no network.
  - **Codex** is the exception. It keeps no usage data on disk at all — the
    desktop app holds threads server-side, and only the CLI and the VS Code
    extension ever wrote rate limits locally — so a disk-fed row silently
    freezes the day you switch to the GUI app. That row is polled from your
    ChatGPT account instead (limits are per account, so it counts GUI usage
    too), authenticated with the OAuth token already in `~/.codex/auth.json`.
    The refresher renews that token when it nears expiry and rewrites the file
    in place, keeping a `.bak`. **If you've never logged into Codex on this
    machine, no call is ever made** — the presence of `auth.json` is the entire
    opt-in.

  Claude's row is *pushed* by its statusline; the Codex and Opencode rows are
  *pulled*, and the pill pulls them itself every few minutes. That matters if
  you don't use Claude: those two rows used to move only while a Claude
  statusline was rendering somewhere, so on a Codex or Opencode machine the pill
  would grey out and stay grey.

  Claude and Opencode each get a **`tokens`** block in that dropdown — raw
  tokens moved today, this week, this month and all time, two periods to a line
  so a full set reads as a 2×2 — and the dropdown closes with an `∑ Everything`
  block adding up every provider:

  ```
  ✳ Claude
    session  48%  ·  resets 11:50
    weekly   13%  ·  resets Sat 03:00
    tokens   220M d  ·  2.02B w
             590M m  ·  7.65B total
  …
  ∑ Everything
    tokens   220M d  ·  2.09B w
             597M m  ·  7.89B total
  ```

  It exists purely for the fun of watching the number climb. Every other figure
  in the pill is a fraction of something you're allowed; this is the count of
  what you've actually moved, which no client shows you anywhere. It's a score,
  not a limit — nothing warns on it, nothing throttles on it, and it never
  reaches the pill's own label, which keeps showing the number you can act on.
  Expect it to be enormous: it counts cache reads alongside real input and
  output, and cache reads are most of what an agent session does.

  A period with nothing in it is dropped rather than printed as a zero, so the
  block just gets smaller — first thing on a Tuesday morning you'll see
  `78.8M w · 7.34M m` over `238M total`, and a provider you haven't used at all
  gets no block. The periods are counted independently, so `w` being *larger*
  than `m` is normal and correct: a week that started in the previous month has
  days in it that this month doesn't. `∑ Everything` appears only when more than
  one provider is reporting — with a single feed it would just restate the block
  above it.

  Claude's is summed from your own transcripts in `~/.claude/projects` — the
  only place the raw counters exist. Reading all of them takes seconds, so an
  index remembers each transcript's size and only re-reads the sessions that
  grew; the score updates roughly every quarter of an hour. Codex has no row
  here for the same reason its limits are polled: no local history to count.

  It is not a bill. Nothing de-duplicates, so a resumed or forked session counts
  the messages it inherited more than once.
- **`elgato`** — a pill that toggles an Elgato Key Light on your local network.
  The light is found for you over mDNS and remembered, so one that takes a new
  address just comes back; pin it with `haus.sill.elgato.host` if you have
  more than one light or mDNS is unreliable on your network. A light it cannot
  reach at all — asleep, off the wifi, flat battery — draws **dim**, which is
  deliberately not the same as the pill's "switched off" look.
- **`harvest`** — a [Harvest](https://www.getharvest.com/) time-tracking pill.
  It reads credentials from a file **you** provide at
  `~/.config/sketchybar/harvest_secrets.sh`.

The **hush** (Do-Not-Disturb) pill is separate: it rides
[`haus.hush.enable`](/guides/hush/), not `sill.items`. It can still move to the
second bar with `haus.sill.bottom.items.hush = true`.

<Aside type="note" title="Secrets stay out of Nix">
sill never bakes tokens into the store. The harvest plugin sources your
`harvest_secrets.sh` at runtime — keep that file out of git. `aiUsage` reads
your Codex login from `~/.codex/auth.json` the same way: at runtime, from the
file OpenAI's own tooling wrote, never through Nix.
</Aside>

## A second bar along the bottom

One bar is the default. If you want more room — or you'd rather keep the
readouts off the menu bar entirely — sill can draw a **second bar along the
bottom of the screen**, running at the same time as the top one:

```nix
haus.sill.bottom = {
  enable = true;
  items = { weather = true; media = true; hush = true; };
};
```

A pill named in `bottom.items` **moves**: it's drawn on the bottom bar and not
on the menu bar, whatever `haus.sill.items` says about it. One switch per pill
per bar, and never two copies of the same readout racing each other.

The set includes the five core pills — `clock`, `weather`, `media`, `battery`,
`wifi` — and the extras: `cpu`, `memory`, `volume`, `calendar`, `caffeinate`,
`agents`, `aiUsage`, `elgato`, `harvest`. Hush can move too when
`haus.hush.enable` is on. The whole left side (workspace pills, front app, the
leader picker) and the tour stay on the menu bar.

Three things worth knowing before you turn it on:

- **macOS reserves the top of your screen for the menu bar and reserves nothing
  at the bottom.** prowl carves the room out instead, so tiled windows stop
  above the new bar — but with `haus.prowl.enable = false` nothing does, and your
  windows will run underneath it.
- **The Dock shares that edge.** Move it to a side, or leave it hidden.
- **Don't put both bars on the same edge.** `haus.sill.position = "bottom"` with
  a second bar means two bars stacked in one place; the rice warns you on
  rebuild rather than quietly drawing one over the other.

Under the hood it really is a second program: SketchyBar draws exactly one bar
per process and names each instance after the binary it was launched as, so the
rice launches the same binary a second time under the name `sill-bottom`. That
name is also how you poke it by hand — `sill-bottom --set cpu label=hello`,
where the top bar takes `sketchybar --set`.

## Turning it off

```nix
haus.sill.enable = false;
```

With sill disabled, the native macOS menu bar is shown again (the rice only
hides it when sill is on) — and the bottom bar goes with it, whatever
`haus.sill.bottom.enable` says. Tiling and the rest of the rice are unaffected.

## Under the hood

sill is generated entirely from your config. On rebuild it writes
`~/.config/sketchybar/` — the main `sketchybarrc`, a `colors.sh` exporting the
nebelung palette as `0xAARRGGBB` values, a `workspaces.sh` built from your app
roster, and a `plugins/` directory of small shell scripts (one per bar item).
The palette and your roster are the single sources of truth, so the bar always
matches the rest of the system.

With a second bar there's a `sill-bottomrc` beside the first rc, and a `bar.sh`
that tells each shared plugin which of the two bars it's talking to — the pills
themselves are the same scripts and each is drawn only on the bar that owns it.
